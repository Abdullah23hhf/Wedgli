Disney+ and Hulu student bundle

This is the one most students are really asking about when they search "Hulu student discount". The details below come from Hulu's student page and Disney+'s student offer page, and the two pages agree.

Bottom line: $6.49 per month for Disney+ and Hulu together, with ads, is the best-confirmed streaming student deal right now. It is also the only one here with no free trial, so you start paying on day one.

What you get

  • Price: $6.49 per month plus tax, against a regular $12.99. Hulu describes this as 50% off the regular monthly price of the bundle.
  • Plan: the ad-supported Disney+ and Hulu bundle only. The pages say the offer is not available for Hulu or Disney+ on their own.
  • Duration: no fixed end date. The price lasts while your enrollment stays verified, "until this offer is discontinued or otherwise unavailable".

Who qualifies

  • US residents aged 18 or older.
  • Students at a US university or an accredited institution that grants post-secondary degrees or certificates.
  • New and existing subscribers. Existing Hulu subscribers who are eligible students can switch to it.
  • Not eligible: ESPN subscribers, and people whose bundle is billed through HBO Max. Only purchases billed directly through Disney+ and Hulu qualify.

How to claim it

  1. Open the Disney+ student offer page and start sign-up.
  2. Create a Disney+ account with your email, a password and your date of birth.
  3. Enter your full school name and a school or personal email address. Verification runs through SheerID.
  4. Once verified, you are charged $6.49 per month plus tax.

The fine print worth reading

  • No free trial. Billing starts immediately.
  • Re-verification every year. After 12 months on the discount you get an email prompt to verify again. If you miss it, you move to the full-priced bundle with ads.
  • No partial refunds. You can cancel any time, but it takes effect at the end of the billing period, and there are no refunds or credits for partial months.
  • Price-lock plans. If you are on a six-month price-lock plan, you can still redeem the offer, but the student price starts only when that plan ends.

Is there a Hulu-only student plan?

Not for new subscribers, according to Hulu. Its student page says new subscribers can only sign up for the bundle, not for separate Hulu or Disney+ student discounts.

That matters because several articles still circulate with a Hulu student price of $1.99 per month. One we found was published in September 2024 and described a Hulu (with ads) plan at $1.99, tied to an upcoming price increase. That is a two-year-old price for a different plan, and it is not what Hulu's page offers today. If a site tells you Hulu is $1.99 for students, check its publish date and then check Hulu directly.

Spotify Premium Student with Hulu

Spotify's student page currently reads: try Spotify Premium Student free for one month, then $6.99 per month. The offer "currently includes access to Hulu (With Ads), subject to eligibility", and is available only to students at an eligible accredited higher education institution who have not tried Premium before.

  • Good for you if: you listen to music daily and only want some TV on the side. You get one bill for both.
  • Watch out for: the Hulu part is described as what the offer "currently" includes, so it can change. Do not count on it as a permanent feature.
  • If you already tried Premium: the page says the offer is for people who have not tried Premium before, so a past trial can rule you out of the free month.

Bundle versus Spotify: the Disney+ and Hulu bundle is $6.49 and gives you two video services. Spotify is $6.99 and gives you music plus Hulu with ads. They are not the same product, but if streaming video is the priority and you already have a free music option, the bundle is the cheaper way to get Hulu.

Peacock, Max, Prime, Apple Music and YouTube Premium

For these five we could not open the official US pages from where we work, so the prices below come from secondary sources and search summaries. They are consistent across what we saw, but confirm on the official page before you enter a card.

Peacock

Reported at $6.99 per month for 12 months on the ad-supported Premium plan, against a regular price of $12.99, verified through SheerID on Peacock's student page. After 12 months you pay the regular price unless you re-verify, and the student rate is not guaranteed for a second year. Some sources list a lower figure, which is one more reason to check the official page.

Max (HBO Max)

Reported at $5.49 per month, which is 50% off the ad-supported Basic plan, with eligibility checked through UNiDAYS. A 2024 report said students keep the price while enrolled but must re-verify every 12 months. Start from the official HBO Max site.

Amazon Prime Student

Reported at $7.49 per month or $69 per year after a six-month free trial, with Prime Video included (with ads). Amazon has also opened the plan to ages 18 to 24 who are not enrolled. Sign up through Amazon's student page. The free trial is the longest of any plan in this guide, but it renews into a paid plan, so set a reminder.

Apple Music Student

$6.99 per month after a free first month for new subscribers, through Apple's student offer page. Apple TV has been included for verified college students, and Apple describes that as a limited-time offer that can end at any time.

YouTube Premium Student

Reported at $8.99 per month in the US after a one-month trial for eligible new members, with yearly student verification, on YouTube's student plan page. It is the most expensive plan here, but it is the only one that removes ads from regular YouTube.

NFL Sunday Ticket: the student plan is gone

NFL Sunday Ticket was one of the more searched student deals this fall, so it is worth being clear. When we checked on November 9, 2026, YouTube's Sunday Ticket offers page showed the "Student Exclusive" card (Get NFL Sunday Ticket for $119) marked Expired. The page had a banner reading "Missed the Student Plan? You can still grab a great deal on NFL Sunday Ticket".

What was live instead was a new-user offer rather than a student offer. The page showed $27 per month for four months, or $108 per year, for new users, against a listed $240. One version of that deal is for Sunday Ticket on YouTube, and another bundles it with the YouTube TV Sports and News plan at an extra $49.99 per month for the first five months, then $71.99 per month. The fine print says availability varies and that an active YouTube TV plan is required for at least one of the offers, with no refunds.

If you are a student who missed the student window, this is the only route on that page, and it is only for people who have never subscribed. Read the terms on the page before you decide, because the YouTube TV version commits you to a much larger monthly bill.

Which one should you pick?

Streaming student plans stack up quickly, so decide what you actually watch before you subscribe to two of them. Here are three setups, using the prices above.

  • TV and movies first: the Disney+ and Hulu bundle at $6.49 per month. Add a music plan only if you really need one.
  • Music first, some TV: Spotify Student at $6.99 per month, with Hulu (with ads) included while the offer lasts.
  • Everything: the Disney+ and Hulu bundle ($6.49) plus Apple Music ($6.99) comes to $13.48 per month. Spotify ($6.99) plus Peacock ($6.99) is $13.98. Max ($5.49) plus the bundle ($6.49) is $11.98. All before tax, and several of those prices are marked reported.

One rule saves money: rotate. Subscribe to one service for the month a show you want comes out, finish it, cancel, and come back later. A student price is only a good deal if you use it. If you do not open the app for two months, the discount did not save you anything.

How verification works

Most streaming student plans verify through SheerID or UNiDAYS. SheerID checks enrollment records, your school sign-in or documents such as a student ID, depending on the school. UNiDAYS is a free student account service that confirms your status and then hands the retailer or service a code.

  • Use your own, personal account for the service rather than a school-issued email that may be deleted when you graduate.
  • Verification usually lasts a year. When it expires, the price moves to the regular rate unless you complete it again.
  • If verification fails, try the school email option, or a different supported document. Contact the service's help center before you create a second account.

Mistakes that cost students money

  • Trusting old prices. The $1.99 Hulu student price is a good example. Always check the publish date and then the official page.
  • Assuming there is a free trial. The Disney+ and Hulu student bundle has none.
  • Missing the re-verification email. The price quietly jumps back to regular.
  • Stacking overlapping services. Hulu shows up in both the bundle and the Spotify offer. Pick one.
  • Buying through a third party. The Disney+ and Hulu student price applies to direct-billed purchases only, so a bundle billed through another company will not qualify.
  • Forgetting to cancel after graduating. Student pricing ends with your enrollment, but billing does not stop on its own.

FAQ

Does Hulu have a student discount?

Yes, but only as part of the ad-supported Disney+ and Hulu bundle. Hulu's student page lists it at $6.49 per month instead of $12.99, verified through SheerID. The page says new subscribers can sign up for the bundle only, not for a Hulu-only or Disney+-only student plan. An older Hulu-only student price of $1.99 appeared in 2024 articles, but it is not what Hulu's page offers now.

How much is the Disney+ and Hulu student bundle?

$6.49 per month plus tax, on the ad-supported bundle, against a regular price of $12.99. There is no free trial. You keep the price while your student status stays verified, and verification repeats every 12 months.

Do I need a .edu email for the Disney+ and Hulu student offer?

Not necessarily. During sign-up you give your full school name and a school or personal email address, and SheerID checks your enrollment. Ages 18 and older at a US university or accredited post-secondary institution qualify.

Which streaming student plans have a free trial?

Based on what we checked, Spotify Premium Student has a one-month free trial, YouTube Premium Student is reported to have one month free, and Amazon's student plan is reported to have six months free. The Disney+ and Hulu student bundle has no free trial.

Is there still a student discount for NFL Sunday Ticket?

Not when we checked on November 9, 2026. YouTube's Sunday Ticket offers page marked the Student Exclusive card ($119) as expired. The page was showing a new-user price of $108 for the rest of the season, which is not a student offer.

What happens to my student price when I graduate?

For the Disney+ and Hulu bundle, if your verification lapses you move to the full-priced bundle with ads. Most other student plans work the same way: they re-verify every year and switch to the regular price when you no longer qualify. Cancel before then if you do not want the regular price.

Which streaming student plan is cheapest?

On listed monthly prices, Max is lowest at a reported $5.49, then the Disney+ and Hulu bundle at $6.49, then Peacock, Spotify and Apple Music at about $6.99. Price is not value, though. The Disney+ and Hulu bundle gives you two services for one price, and Spotify includes music plus Hulu with ads.

Can existing Hulu subscribers get the student price?

Yes, existing Hulu subscribers who are eligible students can get it. ESPN subscribers and people billed for the bundle through HBO Max are not eligible, and only direct-billed purchases through Disney+ and Hulu qualify.
